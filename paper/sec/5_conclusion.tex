\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Image preprocessing is part of the camera model, not merely input plumbing.
\method makes this dependency explicit and tests whether feed-forward 3D
systems respect the resulting equivariance contract.  Across VGGT and DUSt3R
on ETH3D and held-out DTU, two off-center transform families produce a paired
GT orientation failure, while center and symmetric-letterbox controls stay
below threshold.  A support-preserving placement experiment shows that missing
pixels are not necessary: changing only where identical RGB support lies on a
fixed canvas causes large camera drift.  Cross-transform disagreement detects
held-out failures for both models, although it does not beat DUSt3R's native
AUROC.  Canonical-camera warping recovers the cross-dataset rotation gap at zero
measured clean cost, but it is not a general geometry repair.  The practical
lesson is simple: log and compose every pixel map, propagate the induced camera,
and canonicalize known mismatches before trusting reconstructed geometry.
