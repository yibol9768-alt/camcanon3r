\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

Our interventions are controlled affine camera changes, not a measurement of
how often a particular crop occurs in deployed pipelines.  The current
ground-truth evidence uses four fixed views from 13 ETH3D scenes and two model
families; behavior may differ with view count, image content, resolution,
training preprocessing, or other reconstruction systems.  Our ETH3D point-map
metric uses raw scan-supported depth and camera-pose-only alignment and is not
the official MVS leaderboard protocol.  The experiment excludes photometric
corruptions and unrelated-view failures because they do not isolate the known
camera intervention.

Cropping changes coordinates and visible support together.  The canonical
control holds the crop-supported RGB samples fixed and therefore tests whether
camera-canvas placement contributes beyond missing context, but it also adds a
fixed fill and another interpolation step.  It narrows rather than completely
eliminates support, fill, and resampling as alternative explanations.  Our
separately registered edge-letterbox control removes support loss and matches
scale, canvas, and padding count, but was designed after the ETH3D mechanism
results and must be interpreted with that chronology.

Canonicalization can undo coordinates but cannot reconstruct pixels removed by
a crop.  Consistent with that limit, it recovers camera rotation but worsens
depth for both models, and the registered consensus selector is not promoted.
Finally, ETH3D reliability is development evidence because its outcomes were
available when the score was finalized.  Any detector claim requires the
unchanged threshold and score to succeed on held-out DTU; until then,
disagreement remains an audit metric.  DUSt3R additionally carries a
non-commercial code and checkpoint license, so we distribute only our adapter
and protocol.
