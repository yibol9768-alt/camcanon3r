\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Controlled non-equivariance diagnostic}
We run the official VGGT weights on four views from three scenes with seed 17.
Each candidate is compared with the identity prediction using six relative
camera pairs and affine-aware common-support depth consistency.  Table
\ref{tab:pilot} reports disagreement between model runs; it is not a
ground-truth accuracy table.

The asymmetric crop exceeds the pre-registered two-degree rotation threshold
in all three scenes. Its across-scene median is 5.02 degrees with a 95\%
scene-bootstrap interval of [3.79, 6.30] degrees (10,000 percentile
replicates, seed 17). Because this diagnostic contains only three curated
scenes, the interval is descriptive rather than a population-level claim.
Center crops exceed the threshold in only one scene, and square letterbox
padding exceeds it in none. This transform-by-scene interaction is consistent
with a view-specific principal-point mechanism, but severity sweeps and
ground-truth tests are required to distinguish mechanism from correlation.

We repeat the frozen matrix with DUSt3R without pooling models. Asymmetric-crop
median rotation disagreement is 6.33, 8.83, and 5.74 degrees on Kitchen, Fern,
and Room, respectively, exceeding the threshold in all three scenes. Center
crop exceeds it in two scenes, while letterbox exceeds it in none. For the Fern
center crop, DUSt3R collapses all four predicted camera centers to the same
point; translation direction is therefore undefined and excluded from its
scene bootstrap rather than replaced by zero. These measurements support
cross-model non-equivariance in the diagnostic setting, not accuracy loss.

\subsection{Confirmatory ground-truth evaluation}
The frozen confirmatory matrix uses VGGT and DUSt3R on ETH3D
\cite{schops2017eth3d} and DTU~\cite{jensen2014large}.  ETH3D uses four fixed
raw views from each of all 13 training scenes, with matched raw-depth maps.
DTU is held out until all detector scores and thresholds are fixed: we use the
standard 22 MVS evaluation scans, three pixelNeRF~\cite{yu2021pixelnerf} views
(indices 22, 25, and 28), and lighting condition 3.  The same ordered
11-transform severity/scope
matrix is applied to both datasets.  All variants receive pose and intrinsic
evaluation; point accuracy and completeness are computed for the four
predeclared confirmatory variants on DTU and for all variants with available
raw depth on ETH3D.  DUSt3R uses complete symmetrized pairs and its
300-iteration point-cloud optimizer.  Every model-specific resize and crop is
composed with the registered intervention before evaluation.

All confirmatory comparisons are paired by model, scene, view set, and
transform. We aggregate at the scene level and report deterministic 95\%
percentile bootstrap intervals using 10,000 replicates and seed 17. Raw-depth
and pose-only protocols are summarized separately; the evaluator rejects a
mixed aggregate rather than silently treating missing depth as zero.
The raw protocol reports focal and principal-point error, relative pose, depth
AbsRel, and the aligned point-map accuracy/completeness defined in
Sec.~\ref{sec:method}; its result records are versioned so a resumed sweep
cannot reuse outputs from an older metric definition.
Degenerate camera-center alignments remain explicitly undefined and are not
imputed as zero; the corresponding scene metric is excluded from its aggregate
interval while the other GT metrics remain available.

DTU uses the same camera-pose-only orientation-preserving \mbox{Sim(3)} but no
depth metric.  Its point-map evaluation follows the official observation-mask,
ground-plane, and strict 20-mm directional filters with deterministic 0.2-mm
voxelization and a 100,000-point cap.  Because our three-view point maps and
deterministic cap differ from an official MVS submission, we report this as the
\method deterministic DTU point-map metric rather than a leaderboard score.

\paragraph{Matched multi-model results.}
All 104 registered evaluations complete---52 per model---and every pose,
intrinsic, depth, and point-map metric is available.  Table~\ref{tab:eth3d-camera} reports
absolute GT error together with the paired delta from each scene's identity
run.  For VGGT, a view-dependent 75\% crop increases median rotation error by
\SI{4.61}{\degree} (95\% CI [3.13, 6.17]), translation-direction error by
\SI{9.49}{\degree} ([5.06, 13.55]), and depth AbsRel by 2.30 percentage points
([1.19, 2.95]).  Its normalized principal-point error rises by 3.71 percentage
points ([3.68, 3.72]), as expected from independently shifted crop windows.
Focal relative error is 0.0444 with a paired increase of 0.0250
([0.0174, 0.0721]); the camera effect is therefore not confined to a single
reported parameter.

DUSt3R independently shows the same camera/depth failure: asymmetric-crop
rotation increases by \SI{4.52}{\degree} ([2.13, 5.72]), translation by
\SI{10.99}{\degree} ([7.66, 12.37]), depth AbsRel by 1.64 percentage points
([0.27, 2.73]), and normalized principal-point error by 3.69 percentage points
([3.66, 3.70]).  Its focal delta is 0.0227 ([-0.0448, 0.0901]) and therefore
does not support the corresponding VGGT-specific focal claim.

\paragraph{Severity and crop scope.}
We next evaluate the frozen 90/75/60\% retained-fraction matrix, separating a
center crop, one shared off-center window reused across views, and independently
shifted off-center windows (Table~\ref{tab:mechanism}).  Independent cropping
is monotone for both models and crosses two degrees at 75\% and 60\%.  Shared
off-center cropping also crosses at those severities: its 75\% rotation delta
is \SI{2.38}{\degree} ([1.12, 2.77]) for VGGT and
\SI{2.18}{\degree} ([0.88, 2.38]) for DUSt3R.  At 60\%, the deltas reach
\SI{3.71}{\degree} ([2.34, 6.29]) and \SI{3.30}{\degree} ([1.50, 5.56]).

View dependence remains a separate amplifier.  At matched 90/75/60\%
retention, independent-minus-shared rotation deltas are 0.58 ([0.06, 1.33]),
2.01 ([0.29, 4.31]), and 4.37 ([3.54, 7.57]) degrees for VGGT, and 0.41
([0.12, 1.00]), 2.60 ([1.05, 2.99]), and 7.07 ([2.48, 8.55]) degrees for
DUSt3R.  Every interval excludes zero.  Center crops never cross a registered
threshold; DUSt3R's center-crop rotation response is not monotone.  Letterbox
remains a negative control.

The 60\% independent crop also crosses the 0.05 depth-delta threshold for both
models (0.068 and 0.076).  Its point-map alignment is undefined on one VGGT
scene and one DUSt3R scene, so those point metrics are not bootstrapped or
imputed.  The two separate aggregates therefore establish two off-center crop
families on ETH3D, not generic crop sensitivity or cross-dataset generality.
Model-specific non-results remain visible rather than being hidden by pooling.

The canonical-camera repair is paired with each corrupted input and with an
identity clean-cost control.  For an error metric $E$, we report raw errors and
gap recovery
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{Recovery}=
  \frac{E_{\mathrm{corrupt}}-E_{\mathrm{repair}}}
       {E_{\mathrm{corrupt}}-E_{\mathrm{identity}}}.
\end{equation}
The repair claim requires at least 30\% recovery while identity degradation
remains below 2\%; denominators at or below the identity noise floor are
reported as undefined rather than clipped.

\paragraph{Canonical-camera repair.}
Table~\ref{tab:repair} reports the frozen neutral-gray inverse warp on the same
13 ETH3D scenes.  It recovers 0.966 of the paired rotation-error gap for VGGT
(95\% CI [0.729, 1.130]) and 0.558 for DUSt3R ([0.267, 1.060]); both exceed the
0.30 point-estimate gate, although the DUSt3R lower confidence bound does not.
Repeating the identity preparation produces 130/130 byte-exact prediction
arrays for each model, so measured clean rotation degradation is zero.  A
single repaired run takes a median 0.155 seconds per scene for VGGT and 4.80
seconds for DUSt3R, excluding separately reported model loading.

The direct-crop and canonical branches contain the same crop-supported RGB
samples; canonicalization changes their camera-canvas placement and adds only
the registered fill outside that support.  The rotation recovery therefore
makes missing context alone insufficient to explain the direct-crop error.
It does not remove differences from interpolation or filled support, which we
retain as a limitation rather than treating this comparison as a lossless
image transform.

This is an orientation result, not a general geometry repair.  Every
registered fill worsens median depth AbsRel for both models; neutral-gray gap
recovery is $-1.38$ ([$-4.45$, $-0.52$]) for VGGT and $-12.61$

slightly lowers VGGT rotation error from 1.449 to 1.416 degrees, but equals the
2.934-degree neutral baseline for DUSt3R.  It therefore fails the frozen
multi-model promotion rule.  Black fill is a stronger DUSt3R alternative in
hindsight, but cannot replace the predeclared neutral-gray baseline.  We retain
these negative results and promote neither repaired depth nor consensus.

\subsection{Failure detection}
Failure thresholds are frozen before held-out testing and use the strict rule
$E>\tau$. AUROC uses average ranks for ties; risk--coverage adds complete
equal-score blocks and reports raw AURC, oracle AURC, and their difference.
Confidence intervals resample scenes as clusters, carrying all view-set and
transform cases together. Single-class AUROC replicates are omitted and
counted rather than assigned a numerical value.

\paragraph{Development-set pipeline validation.}
On the four-transform ETH3D development matrix, rotation disagreement obtains
AUROC 0.924 (95\% CI [0.816, 1.000]) for VGGT and 0.908 ([0.802, 0.973]) for
DUSt3R, compared with 0.665 ([0.477, 0.865]) and 0.597 ([0.414, 0.795]) for
native uncertainty.  There are 15/52 and 23/52 rotation failures,
respectively.  For the secondary 0.05 depth threshold, disagreement AUROC is
0.900 ([0.785, 0.986]) and 0.797 ([0.551, 1.000]), again above the native
point estimates.  These outcomes were available during score development and
are therefore exploratory; they validate the complete pipeline but do not
promote a detector.  That decision remains reserved for the frozen DTU test.
