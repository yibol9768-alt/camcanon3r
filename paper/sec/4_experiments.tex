\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Controlled non-equivariance diagnostic}
We run the official VGGT weights on four views from three scenes with seed 17.
Each candidate is compared with the identity prediction using six relative
camera pairs and affine-aware common-support depth consistency.  Table
\ref{tab:pilot} reports disagreement between model runs; it is not a
ground-truth accuracy table.

The asymmetric crop exceeds the pre-registered two-degree rotation threshold
in all three scenes. Its across-scene median is 5.02 degrees with a 95\%
scene-bootstrap interval of [3.79, 6.30] degrees (10,000 percentile
replicates, seed 17). Because this diagnostic contains only three curated
scenes, the interval is descriptive rather than a population-level claim.
Center crops exceed the threshold in only one scene, and square letterbox
padding exceeds it in none. This transform-by-scene interaction is consistent
with a view-specific principal-point mechanism, but severity sweeps and
ground-truth tests are required to distinguish mechanism from correlation.

We repeat the frozen matrix with DUSt3R without pooling models. Asymmetric-crop
median rotation disagreement is 6.33, 8.83, and 5.74 degrees on Kitchen, Fern,
and Room, respectively, exceeding the threshold in all three scenes. Center
crop exceeds it in two scenes, while letterbox exceeds it in none. For the Fern
center crop, DUSt3R collapses all four predicted camera centers to the same
point; translation direction is therefore undefined and excluded from its
scene bootstrap rather than replaced by zero. These measurements support
cross-model non-equivariance in the diagnostic setting, not accuracy loss.

\subsection{Confirmatory ground-truth evaluation}
The frozen confirmatory matrix uses VGGT and DUSt3R on ETH3D
\cite{schops2017eth3d}.  We evaluate identity, center crop, asymmetric crop,
and letterbox variants against pose ground truth; raw images additionally use
the matched raw-depth maps, whereas pre-undistorted images are pose-only.
DUSt3R uses four views, complete symmetrized pairs, and its 300-iteration
point-cloud optimizer.  Every model-specific resize and crop is composed with
the registered intervention before evaluation.

All confirmatory comparisons are paired by model, scene, view set, and
transform. We aggregate at the scene level and report deterministic 95\%
percentile bootstrap intervals using 10,000 replicates and seed 17. Raw-depth
and pose-only protocols are summarized separately; the evaluator rejects a
mixed aggregate rather than silently treating missing depth as zero.
The raw protocol reports focal and principal-point error, relative pose, depth
AbsRel, and the aligned point-map accuracy/completeness defined in
Sec.~\ref{sec:method}; its result records are versioned so a resumed sweep
cannot reuse outputs from an older metric definition.
Degenerate camera-center alignments remain explicitly undefined and are not
imputed as zero; the corresponding scene metric is excluded from its aggregate
interval while the other GT metrics remain available.

\paragraph{Matched multi-model results.}
All 104 registered evaluations complete---52 per model---and every pose,
intrinsic, depth, and point-map metric is available.  Table~\ref{tab:eth3d-camera} reports
absolute GT error together with the paired delta from each scene's identity
run.  For VGGT, a view-dependent 75\% crop increases median rotation error by
\SI{4.61}{\degree} (95\% CI [3.13, 6.17]), translation-direction error by
\SI{9.49}{\degree} ([5.06, 13.55]), and depth AbsRel by 2.30 percentage points
([1.19, 2.95]).  Its normalized principal-point error rises by 3.71 percentage
points ([3.68, 3.72]), as expected from independently shifted crop windows.
Focal relative error is 0.0444 with a paired increase of 0.0250
([0.0174, 0.0721]); the camera effect is therefore not confined to a single
reported parameter.

DUSt3R independently shows the same camera/depth failure: asymmetric-crop
rotation increases by \SI{4.52}{\degree} ([2.13, 5.72]), translation by
\SI{10.99}{\degree} ([7.66, 12.37]), depth AbsRel by 1.64 percentage points
([0.27, 2.73]), and normalized principal-point error by 3.69 percentage points
([3.66, 3.70]).  Its focal delta is 0.0227 ([-0.0448, 0.0901]) and therefore
does not support the corresponding VGGT-specific focal claim.

The same failure persists in metric point geometry
(Table~\ref{tab:eth3d-points}).  For VGGT, asymmetric cropping increases
prediction-to-GT accuracy error by \SI{0.259}{\meter} ([0.133, 0.589]) and
GT-to-prediction completeness error by \SI{0.369}{\meter}
([0.161, 0.756]).  DUSt3R completeness increases by \SI{0.416}{\meter}
([0.114, 0.610]), while its \SI{0.252}{\meter} accuracy delta has interval
[-0.015, 0.469] and is not promoted as decisive.  Surface GT is not used to fit
the Sim(3), so these increases cannot be hidden by a geometry-optimized
alignment.

Shared center cropping also reveals a metric-level effect: completeness rises
by \SI{0.145}{\meter} ([0.098, 0.177]) for VGGT and
\SI{0.202}{\meter} ([0.123, 0.362]) for DUSt3R; both accuracy intervals include
zero.  DUSt3R depth AbsRel rises by 0.00868 ([0.00286, 0.0133]), whereas the
VGGT depth interval includes zero.  This does not cross the registered
two-degree pose or five-percentage-point depth threshold for a second transform
family.  Letterbox depth and both point-geometry intervals include zero for
both models.  These negative controls rule out the explanation that any
resampling or tensor-shape change necessarily damages the reconstruction.

The claim boundary follows the frozen controls.  The two separate aggregates
establish multi-model GT degradation under the registered view-dependent crop
on ETH3D raw.  They do not establish generic crop sensitivity, a second
transform family crossing the registered threshold, or cross-dataset
generality.  Model-specific non-results remain visible rather than being
hidden by pooling VGGT and DUSt3R.

The canonical-camera repair is paired with each corrupted input and with an
identity clean-cost control.  For an error metric $E$, we report raw errors and
gap recovery
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{Recovery}=
  \frac{E_{\mathrm{corrupt}}-E_{\mathrm{repair}}}
       {E_{\mathrm{corrupt}}-E_{\mathrm{identity}}}.
\end{equation}
The repair claim requires at least 30\% recovery while identity degradation
remains below 2\%; denominators at or below the identity noise floor are
reported as undefined rather than clipped.  Results remain pending and no
repair claim is drawn from the diagnostic in Table~\ref{tab:pilot} or from an
unfinished baseline.

\subsection{Failure detection and repair}
Failure thresholds are frozen before held-out testing and use the strict rule
$E>\tau$. AUROC uses average ranks for ties; risk--coverage adds complete
equal-score blocks and reports raw AURC, oracle AURC, and their difference.
Confidence intervals resample scenes as clusters, carrying all view-set and
transform cases together. Single-class AUROC replicates are omitted and
counted rather than assigned a numerical value.
