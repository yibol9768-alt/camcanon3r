\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Feed-forward reconstruction models promise to recover cameras and scene
geometry directly from a small set of images, but every practical system first
changes those images to fit a model-specific tensor shape.  Resizing,
cropping, and padding are often treated as implementation details.  In
multi-view geometry they are camera operations: for an image-space affine map
$A$, the projection matrix $P=K[R\mid t]$ becomes $P'=AK[R\mid t]$.  A reliable
system should therefore describe the same scene after this known camera update
is inverted, up to its unavoidable global gauge.

This geometric contract is easy to state but difficult to audit in modern
pipelines.  A user-level crop may be followed by a hidden resize and crop
inside the model; views with different aspect ratios may receive different
maps; and raw predictions cannot be compared until pixel coordinates, camera
conventions, and global scale are reconciled.  Consequently, a change in the
output may be caused by a real equivariance failure or by an incorrect
evaluation map.  Existing corruption tests do not resolve this ambiguity
because they generally treat preprocessing as appearance degradation rather
than a known intervention on the camera.

We introduce \method, an audit protocol for preprocessing-induced geometry
drift.  For every view, \method records the intervention affine, the model's
internal preprocessing affine, and their source-to-tensor composition.  It
then maps predictions to a common image domain and evaluates relative camera
motion, intrinsics, scale-aligned depth, and point geometry using only the
minimal permitted gauge alignment.  The resulting disagreement is measurable
without ground-truth 3D and can therefore serve as a candidate test-time
failure signal; whether it predicts ground-truth error is treated as an
empirical question rather than an assumption.

The ground-truth study isolates a reproducible accuracy failure.  We freeze
four lexicographically selected views from each of all 13 ETH3D training
scenes, apply four registered preprocessing variants, and evaluate 52 complete
VGGT runs.  Relative to paired identity predictions, independently shifted
75\% crops increase median rotation error by \SI{4.61}{\degree} (95\% CI
[3.13, 6.17]), translation-direction error by \SI{9.49}{\degree}
([5.06, 13.55]), depth AbsRel by 0.0230 ([0.0119, 0.0295]), point accuracy
error by \SI{0.259}{\meter} ([0.133, 0.589]), and point completeness error by
\SI{0.369}{\meter} ([0.161, 0.756]).  The normalized principal-point error
increase is 0.0371 ([0.0368, 0.0372]), directly linking the intervention to
the camera mechanism.  Letterbox depth and point-geometry intervals include
zero, while a shared center crop has smaller, metric-dependent effects.  Thus
the evidence supports a view-dependent-crop failure, not generic sensitivity
to all spatial preprocessing.

A separate diagnostic establishes replication boundaries without conflating
cross-run disagreement and GT error.  On three four-view scenes, asymmetric
crops produce 3.79--6.30 degrees median relative-rotation disagreement for
VGGT; an identity repeat is numerically exact.  The frozen DUSt3R replication
yields 5.74--8.83 degrees on the same scenes.  This is a two-model diagnostic
signal, but only VGGT currently has benchmark-scale matched GT evidence.

This draft makes three evidence-bounded contributions:
\begin{itemize}
  \item We formulate image preprocessing as a falsifiable 3D equivariance
        contract with the exact camera update $K' = AK$.
  \item We provide an affine-aware, gauge-invariant evaluation path that
        resolves raw ETH3D calibration per view and evaluates pose, intrinsics,
        depth, and point maps without fitting the surface ground truth.
  \item We establish on 13 ETH3D scenes that view-dependent 75\% cropping
        degrades VGGT cameras, depth, and point geometry, while matched
        letterbox controls remain close to identity; two-model diagnostics
        further delimit the replication target.
\end{itemize}
Matched multi-model and multi-dataset GT results, reliability metrics, and a
training-free consensus repair are pre-registered promotion gates, not
contributions claimed by the present evidence.
