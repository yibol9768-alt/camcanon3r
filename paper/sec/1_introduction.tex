\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Feed-forward reconstruction models recover cameras and scene geometry directly
from a few images, but every practical system first changes those images to fit
a model-specific tensor shape.  Resizing, cropping, and padding are commonly
treated as input plumbing.  In multi-view geometry they are camera operations:
for an image affine $A$, the projection matrix $P=K[R\mid t]$ becomes
$P'=AK[R\mid t]$.  A reliable system should therefore describe the same scene
after this known camera update is removed, up to its unavoidable global gauge.

This contract is easy to state and surprisingly difficult to audit.  A
user-level crop may be followed by a hidden resize or crop inside a model;
different views may receive different maps; and raw predictions cannot be
compared until pixel coordinates, camera conventions, and scale are reconciled.
Consequently, output drift may indicate either a real model failure or an
incorrect evaluation map.  Ordinary corruption tests do not resolve the
ambiguity because they treat preprocessing mainly as appearance degradation,
not as a controlled intervention with a known induced camera.

We introduce \method, an affine-aware audit of preprocessing-induced geometry
drift.  For every view, \method records the intervention affine, the model's
internal preprocessing affine, and their composition.  It maps predictions to
a common image domain and evaluates relative motion, intrinsics, scale-aligned
depth, and point geometry after removing only the permitted global gauge.  The
same machinery yields a test-time score: disagreement between reconstructions
of known equivalent preprocessings.  Whether that score predicts ground-truth
failure is tested on held-out data rather than assumed.

Our confirmatory study contains 770 complete ground-truth evaluations: eleven
registered transforms, VGGT and DUSt3R, all 13 ETH3D training scenes, and 22
held-out DTU evaluation scans.  The principal failure replicates across both
datasets and models.  Independently shifted 75\% crops increase paired median
rotation error by \SI{4.61}{\degree} for VGGT and \SI{4.52}{\degree} for
DUSt3R on ETH3D, and by \SI{5.19}{\degree} and \SI{2.96}{\degree} on DTU.
Translation and principal-point errors rise in the same conditions; on ETH3D,
depth AbsRel increases by 0.0230 and 0.0164 and point completeness by
\SI{0.369}{\meter} and \SI{0.416}{\meter}.  On DTU, VGGT point accuracy and
completeness rise by 2.74 and \SI{2.50}{\milli\meter}.  Strict DTU filtering
makes some DUSt3R point metrics undefined, which we retain rather than impute.

A 90/75/60\% severity and scope sweep identifies two off-center failure
families.  Reusing one off-center window across all views reduces but does not
remove the effect; independently shifting each view amplifies it.  Both
families cross the frozen two-degree threshold on both datasets and models,
whereas center crops and symmetric letterbox do not.  We then remove the
strongest remaining alternative explanation.  A separately registered control
places every source RGB pixel, byte-for-byte and at scale one, at centered,
shared-edge, or independently sampled positions on the same square canvas.
Independent placement raises paired rotation error by 5.69--13.97 degrees in
all four model--dataset combinations.  Thus missing support is not necessary:
image-canvas coordinates themselves causally change the recovered cameras.

The audit signal also survives a held-out reliability test.  On four frozen
DTU cases per scan, disagreement detects rotation failures with AUROC 0.998
(95\% CI [0.990, 1.000]) for VGGT and 0.855 ([0.732, 0.949]) for DUSt3R.  It
beats the VGGT native score (0.647) but ties the DUSt3R native AUROC (0.855), so
we claim a useful detector, not universal superiority to model confidence.
Finally, inverse-warping a known crop to its canonical camera canvas recovers
94.1\% and 76.7\% of the held-out rotation-error gap for VGGT and DUSt3R with
zero measured clean cost.  The repair also works for ETH3D orientation, but
ETH3D depth worsens and some DTU point aggregates remain undefined; our repair
claim is therefore deliberately restricted to camera orientation.

Our contributions follow the same claim--mechanism--evidence--boundary pattern
used by strong recent geometry systems:
\begin{itemize}
  \item We formulate preprocessing as a falsifiable 3D equivariance contract
        with the exact induced camera update $K'=AK$.
  \item We provide an affine-aware, gauge-invariant protocol with frozen
        complete-design checks, ground-truth camera/depth/point evaluation,
        scene-cluster uncertainty, and auditable undefined values.
  \item We establish a two-model, two-dataset off-center coordinate failure and
        isolate it with severity, shared-window, center, letterbox, repeat, and
        support-preserving placement controls.
  \item We validate cross-transform disagreement as a held-out failure detector
        and canonical-camera warping as a cross-dataset orientation repair,
        while retaining the native-score tie and geometry failures as explicit
        limits.
\end{itemize}
