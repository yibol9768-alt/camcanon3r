\begin{abstract}
Feed-forward 3D reconstruction systems resize, crop, and pad their inputs, even
though these operations change the image formation model.  An image affine
$A$ changes the camera from $K[R\mid t]$ to $AK[R\mid t]$; after accounting for
this known update, the recovered scene should remain unchanged up to global
gauge.  We formulate this requirement as a preprocessing-equivariance contract
and introduce an auditable protocol that composes user and model pixel maps and
evaluates cameras, intrinsics, depth, and point geometry against ground truth.
Across 770 registered evaluations of VGGT and DUSt3R on 13 ETH3D and 22 held-out
DTU scenes, independently shifted 75\% crops increase paired median rotation
error by \SI{4.61}{\degree} and \SI{4.52}{\degree} on ETH3D, and by
\SI{5.19}{\degree} and \SI{2.96}{\degree} on DTU.  A severity/scope sweep finds
a second shared off-center failure family on both datasets, while center crops
and symmetric letterbox stay below the frozen threshold.  More decisively,
moving unchanged RGB support to independently sampled positions on the same
canvas raises rotation error by 5.69--13.97 degrees in all four model--dataset
combinations, ruling out support loss as a necessary cause.  On held-out DTU,
cross-transform disagreement detects $>\!2$-degree failures with AUROC 0.998
for VGGT and 0.855 for DUSt3R; it strongly exceeds native confidence for VGGT
but ties it for DUSt3R.  Known-affine canonicalization recovers 0.941 and 0.767
of the DTU rotation-error gap with zero measured clean cost.  Thus image-canvas
coordinates are a causal systems variable for modern feed-forward 3D models;
the supported repair claim is limited to camera orientation, not generic
geometry recovery.
\end{abstract}
