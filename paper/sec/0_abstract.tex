\begin{abstract}
Feed-forward 3D reconstruction systems routinely resize, crop, and pad their
input images, although these operations change the image formation model.  An
image-space affine transform $A$ changes the camera matrix from $K[R\mid t]$
to $AK[R\mid t]$; after this known update is accounted for, the recovered
scene should remain unchanged up to the model's global gauge.  We formulate
this requirement as a preprocessing-equivariance contract and introduce an
auditable protocol that records both user-level and model-internal image
transforms, composes their exact pixel maps, and compares reconstructed
cameras, intrinsics, depth, and point geometry against ground truth.  Across
13 ETH3D scenes and 52 complete VGGT evaluations, independently shifted 75\%
crops increase median rotation and translation-direction error by
\SI{4.61}{\degree} and \SI{9.49}{\degree}, respectively, relative to paired
identity runs.  They also increase depth AbsRel by 2.30 percentage points and
point-cloud accuracy and completeness error by \SI{0.259}{\meter} and
\SI{0.369}{\meter}; all five scene-bootstrap intervals exclude zero.  In
contrast, letterbox depth and point-geometry deltas are statistically
indistinguishable from identity.  A frozen three-scene DUSt3R diagnostic
reproduces the cross-run rotation signal, but matched DUSt3R ground-truth
evaluation remains the gate for cross-model promotion.  The evidence therefore
establishes a specific VGGT accuracy failure under view-dependent cropping,
not generic fragility to every resize or crop; failure detection and repair
remain separate empirical claims.
\end{abstract}
