\begin{abstract}
Feed-forward 3D reconstruction systems routinely resize, crop, and pad their
input images, although these operations change the image formation model.  An
image-space affine transform $A$ changes the camera matrix from $K[R\mid t]$
to $AK[R\mid t]$; after this known update is accounted for, the recovered
scene should remain unchanged up to the model's global gauge.  We formulate
this requirement as a preprocessing-equivariance contract and introduce an
auditable protocol that records both user-level and model-internal image
transforms, composes their exact pixel maps, and compares reconstructed
cameras and depth on common source-image support.  In a controlled four-view
VGGT diagnostic across three scenes, independently shifted 75\% crops produce
median relative-rotation disagreement of \SIrange{3.79}{6.30}{\degree}, while
an identity-repeat control is numerically exact.  A frozen DUSt3R replication
independently yields \SIrange{5.74}{8.83}{\degree} on the same scenes.  Center
crops and letterbox padding are more scene dependent, implicating inconsistent
principal-point shifts rather than cropping alone.  These results establish a
reproducible two-model non-equivariance signal; ground-truth degradation,
failure detection, and repair remain explicit confirmatory gates.
\end{abstract}
